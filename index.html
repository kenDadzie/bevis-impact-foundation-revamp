Zambia’s 95-95-95

The UNAIDS goal: 95% of people with HIV know their status, 95% of them are on treatment, and 95% of those have the virus under control.

98%

of those who know are on treatment.

96%

of those on treatment have the virus suppressed.

Young people are furthest behind. Among 15 to 24-year-olds living with HIV, about 7 in 10 have the virus suppressed, against 86% of all adults.

Source: ZAMPHIA 2021 summary sheet, adults 15+, Zambia Ministry of Health with CDC and ICAP, December 2022. Figures rounded from 88.7%, 98.0% and 96.3%.

In honour of Bevis Phiri

Bevis Impact Foundation is a youth-focused foundation in Zambia. We promote HIV testing, sexual health awareness, safer sex and stigma reduction, and we push for HIV prevention and care that young people actually want to use.

Our name honours Bevis Phiri. His work in HIV research and service delivery inspires ours: services that are effective, youth-friendly and good for the people who use them and the staff who run them.

We picture a Zambia where every young person knows their status and no one is left behind in prevention or care. Our work is youth-centred, compassionate, evidence-led and measured by impact: shaped by community needs, informed by research and delivered without judgment.

What we do

  • Know your status

    Youth-friendly HIV testing campaigns, pop-up community events and school outreach, with self-testing access on the way. The aim is simple: make knowing your status normal.

  • Sexual health education

    Clear, relatable sessions on safer sex, condoms, consent, stigma, HIV prevention and sexual wellbeing, for young people across Zambia.

  • Bevis Impact Lab

    Research-informed service delivery reviews, youth feedback and community insight, so HIV services work better for the people who use them and the staff who run them.

Get tested

Knowing your status is an act of strength. Here is what happens, from your first message to your result, so nothing about it comes as a surprise.

  1. Ask us

    Send a request with the form below: your province and what you need. We get back to you and help you plan the next step.

  2. Find a place

    We link you to a youth-friendly clinic or a community outreach event near you. Self-testing access is on the way.

  3. Talk it through

    A trained counsellor explains the test in private and answers your questions. Nothing happens until you are ready.

  4. The test

    A small finger-prick of blood, or a mouth swab with a self-test. The result is ready in about 20 minutes.

  5. Next steps

    Negative: we help you stay that way, with condoms, PrEP and regular tests. Positive: you get a confirmatory test and can start treatment straight away. Treatment keeps you healthy and stops HIV passing on.

Myth or fact?

Six things people say about HIV. Make your call on each one, then read the answer.

Your score: 0 of 6

  • You can tell by looking

    Myth. Most people with HIV look and feel well for years. A test is the only way to know.

  • Kissing spreads HIV

    Myth. HIV is not passed on through saliva, hugs, sharing cups or toilet seats. It spreads through sex without a condom, shared needles, and from mother to baby without treatment.

  • Condoms stop HIV

    Fact. Used correctly every time, condoms are highly effective against HIV and other sexually transmitted infections.

  • One test is enough

    Myth. A test shows your status at that moment. Test again after any risk, and at least once a year if you are sexually active.

  • Meds stop the spread

    Fact. When HIV treatment keeps the virus undetectable, HIV is not passed on through sex. Undetectable means untransmittable.

  • HIV is the end

    Myth. With daily treatment, people living with HIV live long, healthy lives, work, have relationships and have children.
